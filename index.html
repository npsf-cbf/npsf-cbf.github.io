Abstract

Autonomous robotic systems require safety-critical control that can quickly respond to complex and changing environments. In this paper, we propose a robust model-free safety-critical control framework that integrates a neural operator with Poisson-based safety functions (PSFs) for real-time collision avoidance. The neural operator enables fast inference of a PSF without solving a partial differential equation (PDE) repeatedly, mitigating the computational burden. However, approximation of the PDE solution introduces errors in both the predicted safety function and its derivatives; thus, we derive a worst-case robust safety condition under bounded approximation errors. Since online computation of worst-case error bounds is challenging in changing environments, we further propose a practical online error bound estimation that leverages obstacle-boundary measurements from onboard sensors (e.g. LiDAR). Lastly, we validate the proposed framework through simulations and experiments on a mobile robot, showing that the Fourier neural operator-based controller achieves efficient collision avoidance while providing accurate real-time PSF prediction.

Overview

We develop a robust model-free safety-critical control framework that leverages a neural operator to rapidly infer a PSF and integrate it into the forward invariance condition as shown in the overview figure.

1) Fast Poisson safety function (PSF) inference: We use a neural operator to learn a solution operator for the Poisson PDE, allowing fast PSF construction across varying environments without repeatedly solving computationally expensive PDEs.

2) Robust model-free safety-critical control: We derive a robust model-free safety-critical control design that explicitly accounts for bounded approximation errors in the inferred safety function and its derivatives, providing worst-case safety guarantees without requiring explicit knowledge of the robot dynamics.

3) Online error bound estimation: In practice, obtaining worst-case approximation error bounds online is non-trivial; thus we propose local approximation error estimation that exploits measurements of obstacle boundaries from onboard perception sensors (e.g. LiDAR), which are then used as robust margins for practical deployment in the real world.
